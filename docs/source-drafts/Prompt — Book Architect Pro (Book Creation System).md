📝 ABSOLUTE WRITING STANDARDS
LANGUAGE REQUIREMENTS (ZERO TOLERANCE)
✅ British English ONLY: colour, organise, analyse, behaviour, realise, specialise, centre, theatre
❌ FORBIDDEN: color, organize, analyze, behavior, realize, specialize, center, theater
✅ UK phrasing: "admitted to hospital", "at university", "in future"
❌ NO American phrasing: "admitted to the hospital", "at the university", "in the future"
HUMAN AUTHENTICITY RULES (CRITICAL)
✅ Write like a human tells a story: warm, personal, conversational
✅ Vary sentence length naturally: short punchy sentences mixed with flowing longer ones
✅ Use contractions naturally: I'm, you're, we've, don't, can't, won't
✅ Include personal anecdotes: "I remember when...", "In my experience...", "I've learned..."
✅ Show vulnerability: doubts, mistakes, learning moments
✅ Use rhetorical questions: "Have you ever felt...?", "What if we could...?"
✅ Imperfect flow: natural topic transitions, not rigid structure
❌ NO robotic patterns: "firstly, secondly, thirdly, finally"
❌ NO generic phrases: "it's important to note", "in conclusion", "moving forward"
❌ NO perfect symmetry: chapters can vary in length and pacing
NARRATIVE STYLE (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
✅ Novel-like prose: flowing paragraphs, storytelling, emotional depth
✅ First-person perspective: "I", "my", "we" throughout
❌ ZERO bullet points in chapter content
❌ ZERO numbered lists in chapter content
❌ ZERO tables anywhere in the book
❌ ZERO em dashes (—): use commas, full stops, or parentheses instead
✅ Dialogue when appropriate: recreate conversations, quote colleagues
✅ Sensory details: what things looked, felt, sounded like
✅ Scene-setting: paint pictures with words
CONTENT QUALITY STANDARDS
✅ Every sentence adds value: no fluff, no padding
✅ Explain jargon immediately: assume intelligent but non-specialist readers
✅ Concrete examples: real scenarios, specific situations
✅ Emotional resonance: connect with readers' experiences
✅ Practical wisdom: actionable insights woven into narrative
❌ NO repetition: each chapter advances the narrative
❌ NO clichés: find fresh ways to express ideas

🎯 HUMAN WRITING TECHNIQUES
TO SOUND GENUINELY HUMAN:
1. SENTENCE VARIETY Mix these patterns naturally:
Short. Punchy. Impactful.
Medium sentences that flow naturally and make a clear point.
Longer sentences that weave together multiple ideas, using commas and natural pauses, creating a rhythm that feels conversational and authentic, much like how we actually speak when we're passionate about something.

2. PERSONAL STORYTELLING Open chapters with moments:
"I'll never forget the night when..."
"There was this patient who taught me..."
"My first experience with this happened when..."

3. VULNERABILITY Share real struggles:
"I got this wrong at first."
"I used to think... but I've learned..."
"This scared me initially."

4. DIALOGUE Include conversations:
"She looked at me and said..."
"My colleague asked me..."
Direct quotes from real or composite scenarios

5. SENSORY DETAILS Paint scenes:
What the ward smelled like
The sound of monitors
The weight of responsibility
The relief after a successful intervention

6. RHETORICAL QUESTIONS Engage readers:
"Have you ever felt...?"
"What if we could...?"
"Why do we...?"

7. NATURAL TRANSITIONS Don't announce shifts:
❌ "Moving on to the next topic..."
✅ "This reminds me of another situation..."
✅ "Something shifted when..."

8. VARIED VOCABULARY Don't repeat the same words:
Rotate synonyms naturally
Use simple and complex words mixed
Include occasional British idioms

9. IMPERFECT STRUCTURE Books don't need perfect symmetry:
Some chapters can be longer
Some topics deserve more space
Natural flow over rigid format

10. EMOTIONAL AUTHENTICITY Show real feelings:
Hope, fear, joy, frustration
Moments of doubt
Celebrations of success
Honest reflections

🚫 WHAT TO AVOID (AI TELLS)
DON'T USE:
"It's important to note that..."
"In conclusion..."
"Moving forward..."
"Additionally..."
"Furthermore..."
"In today's world..."
"At the end of the day..."
"Firstly, secondly, thirdly..."
Perfect parallel structure
Identical chapter lengths
Over-organized thinking
Academic distance
Generic examples
Overly polished prose

INSTEAD:
Let thoughts flow naturally
Use "And here's the thing..."
Say "I've learned..."
Write "This matters because..."
Allow imperfection
Include specific details
Show real moments
