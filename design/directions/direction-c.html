The Calm Ward

De-escalation and Compassion in NHS Practice

HMHarni Muharami blueprint version 3, today at 14:12

Summary

Every shift on a busy ward holds moments that could tip into conflict: a frightened relative, a patient in pain, a colleague at the end of a long night. From years on wards and in the emergency department, I show how to read distress early, talk people down with dignity and learn from every incident without blame.

Target audience

Ward nurses, healthcare assistants and student nurses in NHS settings, and the ward managers, matrons and educators who shape safer teams.

4

The Art Of Verbal De-escalation

Opens with a scene

A son at the nurses' station at three in the morning, voice rising, demanding a doctor. This chapter slows that moment down: what the body says before the words do, how the talk down approach works in practice, when stepping back is the safest choice, and how to stay calm through an interpreter.
