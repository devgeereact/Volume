<section epub:type="chapter" class="chapter" id="chapter-1-the-light-comes-back"><header><p class="kicker">Chapter 1</p><h1>The Light Comes Back</h1></header><p class="noindent">The lamp at Gull Point had been dark for eleven years when Morwenna Tregear saw it burning from her kitchen window.</p>
<p>She set down the kettle and watched. The beam swung once across the bay, slow and yellow, and then it was gone.</p>
<p>&quot;You saw it too,&quot; said her brother from the doorway. He had his coat on already.</p>
<hr class="break"/>
<p class="noindent">The path to the point was slick with rain. Morwenna went first, torch in one hand, the other on the cold iron rail her father had fixed to the rock.</p>
<p>At the top the door of the lamp room stood open. Someone had swept the floor.</p></section>

<section epub:type="chapter" class="chapter" id="chapter-2-footprints-in-the-salt"><header><p class="kicker">Chapter 2</p><h1>Footprints in the Salt</h1></header><p class="noindent">By morning the whole village had an opinion. Mrs Kitto at the shop said it was boys from Penzance. The vicar said nothing at all, which Morwenna noticed.</p>
<p>She went back up alone after lunch. The floor was still clean, but a line of salty footprints led from the stairs to the window and stopped.</p>
<hr class="break"/>
<p class="noindent">Her brother was waiting at the bottom of the path when she came down.</p>
<p>&quot;Well?&quot; he said.</p>
<p>&quot;Whoever it was,&quot; she said, &quot;they never came back down.&quot;</p></section>
